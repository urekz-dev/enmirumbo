# ADR 0011: Photos in R2, transformed on upload with Cloudflare Images

- Status: accepted
- Date: 2026-10-11
- Owner: Alexis Zazueta

## Context

The prototype resized photos with sharp, which does not run on Workers, and stored them in a private Supabase bucket served through a route of its own. Cloudflare Images offers a Workers binding with 5,000 unique transformations per month free; reading image metadata is free. R2 buckets can be exposed on a custom hostname cached by the CDN with no Worker invocation.

## Decision

The registration form uploads the photo to the Worker, which validates type and size, transforms it once with the Images binding into two WebP sizes (card and full) with metadata stripped, and stores only those in R2. Originals are not kept. Each environment has its own bucket and hostname: `fotos.enmirumbo.com`, `fotos-stage.enmirumbo.com`, `fotos-dev.enmirumbo.com` (amended on 2026-10-18: one public media bucket for production and one for `dev` and `stage`, with temporary hostnames under `urekz.com`). Keys are opaque and generated by the server; a photo of an unpublished business is not referenced anywhere public.

Amended on 2026-10-18 (EMR-35), by decision of the product owner:

- Photos live in a general public media bucket, not a photos bucket. It holds only public content that people or the admin upload, organized by prefix; business photos live under `photos/`, as `photos/<photo key>/card.webp` (400×400, the listing card's) and `photos/<photo key>/full.webp` (1200×900, the card's). Anything private, such as the backups, stays in a bucket of its own. Everything is named "media", never "assets", which in this repository means the site's static files.
- There are two media buckets, not three: `enmirumbo-media` for production alone, and `enmirumbo-media-dev`, which `dev` and `stage` share. A Worker's R2 binding and an R2 API token cannot be scoped to a prefix, so production must not be reachable from `dev`, which deploys any branch; and `dev` resets its database from `stage`, so sharing the bucket keeps the cards `dev` copies pointing at photos that exist. The Worker binds the bucket as `MEDIA`, and the variable that names the media hostname is `MEDIA_URL` (it was `PHOTOS_URL`).
- Until `enmirumbo.com` is on Cloudflare (EMR-63), the media hostnames are temporary ones under `urekz.com`, whose zone is: `media-enmirumbo.urekz.com` for production and `media-dev-enmirumbo.urekz.com` for `dev` and `stage`, later `media.enmirumbo.com` and `media-dev.enmirumbo.com`. Moving them is a change of configuration (`MEDIA_URL` and the custom domains), never of code.
- Cloud resources are named in English. A hostname uses subdomains while it stays one level below its zone, because Cloudflare's free Universal SSL certificate covers only the zone and one level of subdomain; deeper levels are flattened with hyphens, so the ideal `media.dev.enmirumbo.urekz.com` is `media-dev-enmirumbo.urekz.com` under `urekz.com`, and `media-dev.enmirumbo.com` under `enmirumbo.com`.
- Each object carries `Cache-Control: public, max-age=31536000, immutable`, which Cloudflare's cache follows on the media hostname: a key never changes its bytes. The `r2.dev` URL of both buckets stays off, and the media hostname never lists a bucket.
- Locally `MEDIA_URL` is empty and the local Worker serves Miniflare's local bucket on its own origin under `/_media/`, only with `ENVIRONMENT` `local` and on a loopback host. The fictitious seed draws its photos when it runs and writes them to the local bucket, or, on the deploys to `dev` and `stage`, to the shared bucket through R2's S3 API with a token scoped to it.

## Consequences

Two transformations per photo, so the free allowance covers far more uploads than expected. Listing pages do not spend Worker requests on images. Photos of businesses that are deleted are removed before the row, and an orphan sweep runs in the scheduled job. The sweep runs on `stage` and production, each against its environment's bucket and database; `dev` has no Cron Trigger. Because `dev` and `stage` share a bucket, a photo uploaded on `dev` that the `stage` database does not name is swept once its objects are older than seven days. The product owner accepts this because `dev` is reset from `stage` on every deploy. Production uses the same seven-day grace, so an upload in flight is never swept.

Amended on 2026-10-18 (EMR-40), by decision of the product owner: a photo costs three transformations, not two. Images opens the original once, into a working copy scaled down into 2400×2400, and both variants are cut from that copy (`fit: cover` from the centre), so neither is ever cut from the original again and both crops come from the whole photo; a variant over its weight is compressed again from the copy, one more transformation each time. The free 5,000 unique transformations a month still cover well over a thousand photos; past them Images answers error 9422 and charges nothing, and the form asks for another photo or to register without one.

Amended on 2026-10-18 (EMR-48), by decision of the product owner: because every object carries a year of caching, deleting a photo also purges both of its addresses from Cloudflare's cache of the media hostname, with the purge by URL of its zone and a token that may only purge that zone (the Worker secrets `MEDIA_ZONE_ID` and `MEDIA_CACHE_PURGE_TOKEN`). Every deletion of a photo goes through the one function that deletes its objects and then purges it, so none can leave the purge out. A purge that fails keeps only the photo's two addresses, in the table `photo_cache_purges`, and the hourly job retries them (ADR 0013); the permanent deletion then says that the photo may still be seen a while. Locally there is no cache of Cloudflare's in front of the bucket, and nothing is purged.

Amended on 2026-10-19 (EMR-57), by decision of the product owner: the orphan sweep gives every environment seven days before it deletes an unnamed photo, including production. It runs on `stage` and production, never on `dev`; on the shared `dev` and `stage` bucket, `stage` may therefore delete a photo uploaded on `dev` if its own database does not name it. This is accepted because `dev` is reset from `stage` on every deploy.
